The door is open.

Four days ago Ethan Reed was an ordinary maintenance technician. Now his wristband says G‑37, the power has failed five levels underground, and his cell door has been left open a few inches.

01 / The game

Think first.
Then run.

The Kestrel Deep Laboratory has lost control of its contained SCPs. Get through it with what a technician has: a working knowledge of wiring, valves and machines, and whatever the room is trying to tell you.

01

Every puzzle has an insight

The answer is already in the scene, waiting for one realization only you can reach. Solving it visibly changes the world: power, doors, light or a pursuer's position.

02

SCP behavior can be learned

Each SCP's rule is shown before it can kill you, so a chase is about using that rule under pressure. Not every SCP is hostile, and some moments are a welcome relief.

03

Escape has a human cost

People help Ethan and pay for it. He can choose to help them in return, and those choices decide who is with him at the end, and which ending he reaches.

02 / The way up

Five levels.
Ten acts.

Each act is built around a demanding puzzle or a chase, and is designed to last about five to ten minutes. Start in the cells at the bottom of the laboratory and climb toward the last door.
